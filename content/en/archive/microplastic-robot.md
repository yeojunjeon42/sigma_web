## Overview

Bivalves such as scallops accumulate large amounts of microplastics over their life cycle. Inspired by this, our team ("Ppup-ppup") developed a low-power biomimetic robot modeled on a scallop to collect marine microplastics. We 3D-printed a hydrodynamically shaped shell and built a motor-driven opening-and-closing mechanism that mimics how scallops swim by drawing water in and expelling it backward. An ultrasonic sensor detects surrounding obstacles. This low-power biomimetic platform can collect microplastics without harming marine ecosystems and could be networked in swarms for broader underwater monitoring and cleanup tasks.

## Motivation

Our goal was to develop and evaluate a simple, low-power biomimetic underwater robot platform capable of collecting microplastics as well as performing other surface and underwater tasks.

## How it works

The robot consists of two hinged shells, a motor and crankshaft drive, a front intake membrane, an Arduino Nano, an ultrasonic sensor for forward obstacle detection, and a 12V battery pack made of 1.5V cells wired in series. In water tests, the robot advanced approximately 5 cm per open-close cycle at an average speed of 10 cm/s, demonstrating effective propulsion from a simple drive mechanism.

## Process

We first studied scallop locomotion: a scallop opens its shell to draw in water and snaps it shut to jet water backward for propulsion. This simple mechanism facilitates compact sizing and straightforward fabrication. We 3D-printed the shell and hinge assembly using ABS plastic to keep the structure lightweight despite its volume, controlled the robot with a compact Arduino Nano, and kept the firmware simple to minimize underwater failures.

## Impact

Existing microplastic removal methods such as membrane bioreactors (MBR) and electrocoagulation suffer from low efficiency or secondary environmental impacts. In contrast, biomimetic scallop robots avoid harming marine life and can be scaled up easily in numbers. Future upgrades could add LiDAR sensors to map microplastic concentrations and replace electric motors with soft pneumatic artificial muscles to further reduce environmental impact.
