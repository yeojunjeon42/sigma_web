## Overview

Syncope (fainting) has many causes and can affect anyone; 3% to 5% of healthy adults experience syncope at least once in their lifetime. Because triggers such as tension and stress are hard to control, rather than focusing on prevention or medical treatment, we focused on establishing engineering criteria to detect fainting and mechanically protect patients from fall-related trauma so people with chronic syncope can go about their daily lives safely. Since physical impacts during a fall fall outside standard clinical pathology, we designed a mechanical safety device to protect users from injury.

## Motivation

This project was created to prevent physical trauma from fainting while walking and to reduce the psychological anxiety caused by potential falls. According to Severance Hospital data on vasovagal syncope, 3% to 5% of healthy adults faint at least once in their lives. Patients who experience recurrent syncope due to cardiovascular or other conditions risk severe injuries during sudden episodes and may be unable to call for help if alone. We aimed to develop a safety support system that prevents collision trauma during a fall and automatically sends syncope logs and location alerts via a mobile app. By designing the device as an everyday bag, we created an unobtrusive safety aid that activates in emergencies.

## How it works

This product offers advantages in both functionality and design. Functionally, it combines two detection metrics—muscle relaxation and fall motion recognition—to trigger an airbag and emergency app notifications, directly and indirectly protecting patients with chronic syncope. Design-wise, packaging the system inside a regular wearable bag allows users who never know when an episode might occur to carry personal belongings as usual while staying protected.

## Process

For primary syncope detection, we used motion recognition via an accelerometer. Machine learning applied to three-axis acceleration data distinguishes normal activities—such as walking, standing still, and jumping—from the falling motion characteristic of fainting.[1] As a secondary check, we collected electromyography (EMG) signals to detect sudden muscle relaxation, preventing intentional falls or drops from being misclassified as syncope.

For primary trauma protection, we used an airbag mechanism. Adapting an auto-inflating life vest mechanism with a carbon dioxide cylinder, the airbag deploys mechanically upon syncope detection. As a secondary measure, the device pairs with a mobile application to notify designated emergency contacts with the user's location so they can receive timely medical assistance.

## Impact

Many safety devices suffer from low adoption rates due to the discomfort of continuous wear. According to a 2019 Yonhap News report, bicycle helmet usage among children and adolescents was below 5%. Designing our safety system as an everyday bag ensures practicality and encourages consistent daily use.
